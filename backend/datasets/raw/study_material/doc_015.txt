CHAPTER 4: PRIMARY KEY

Primary Key
Primary key is a column or set of columns that uniquely identifies
every row in a table. Note that primary key depends on the
assumptions made about the problem. A common mistake is to confuse
primary key with deadlock.

Indexing
Indexing is a structure that speeds up the retrieval of rows by
searching on selected columns. In practice, indexing is combined
with deadlock to solve larger problems. The main advantage of
indexing is that it is simple to understand and efficient in
practice.

Normalization:
Normalization is the process of organising tables to reduce
redundancy and update anomalies. Students should remember that
normalization is closely related to transaction.

Deadlock
Deadlock is a situation in which two transactions wait for each
other to release locks. For example, consider a small case in
Database Management Systems where deadlock is applied step by step.

Key points
- Remember the definition of primary key.
- Indexing is related to deadlock.

Summary
In this unit we studied primary key, indexing, normalization and
deadlock. These ideas form the foundation for further topics in
Database Management Systems.
