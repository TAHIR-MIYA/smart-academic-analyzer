Isothermal Process - Study Notes

Learning objectives
- Define isothermal process and explain where it is used.
- Define enthalpy and explain where it is used.

Isothermal Process
Isothermal process is a process that takes place at constant
temperature. Note that isothermal process depends on the assumptions
made about the problem. Students should remember that isothermal
process is closely related to entropy. A common mistake is to confuse
isothermal process with entropy.

Enthalpy:
Enthalpy is the total heat content of a system at constant pressure.
A common mistake is to confuse enthalpy with isothermal process. Note
that enthalpy depends on the assumptions made about the problem.
Students should remember that enthalpy is closely related to
isothermal process.

Heat Engine:
Heat engine is a device that converts heat energy into useful
mechanical work. For example, consider a small case in Thermodynamics
where heat engine is applied step by step. In practice, heat engine
is combined with isothermal process to solve larger problems. A
common mistake is to confuse heat engine with isothermal process.

Ideal Gas:
Ideal gas is a hypothetical gas whose molecules have no volume and no
mutual attraction. In practice, ideal gas is combined with isothermal
process to solve larger problems. Note that ideal gas depends on the
assumptions made about the problem.

Summary
In this unit we studied isothermal process, enthalpy, heat engine and
ideal gas. These ideas form the foundation for further topics in
Thermodynamics.
